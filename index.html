Where the ideal lines come from

They're simple, published benchmarks. Treat them as a guide, not a verdict.

Strength: bodyweight multiples

Strength tables express a good lift as a multiple of your bodyweight. A typical beginner man benches about half his bodyweight, and an intermediate lifter about 1.25 times. The ideal line starts at the beginner level for your weight and moves toward intermediate over roughly a year of steady training. Women's standards are lower, and we use those when your profile says so.

Sources: arvo.guru, Coachway, Strength Level. They disagree on exact numbers, so ours are middle-of-the-road.

Weight: the calorie rule of thumb

About 7,700 kcal of surplus or deficit is roughly 1 kg of body weight. We add up your daily balance from your first weigh-in to draw the predicted line. If the real line drifts away from it, your logged calories are probably off, which is useful to know.

It's an average. Water, salt and muscle mean your weight won't follow it exactly day to day.

Cinetic is a tracking tool, not medical advice. Check with a professional before big changes to your training or diet.

Questions

Do I need an account?

Not for this version. Everything is saved in your own browser, and nothing is sent anywhere.

What happens if I clear my browser data?

Your logs go with it, because they live only in the browser for now. Accounts that keep your data across devices are the next thing planned.

Can I use pounds?

Yes. Switch between kg and lb on either page, and every number converts.

What exercises can I track?

Bench press, squat, deadlift and overhead press come with an ideal line. You can also add any other exercise by name, and it gets its own chart.

Why is my weight asked on the diet page only?

You weigh in once a day, so it lives in one place. The gym page reads your latest weight to build your ideal lines.

Ready to see your progress?

Pick a side to start. It takes a minute to set up, and the other page is one tap away.
